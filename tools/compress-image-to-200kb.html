How to Reduce an Image to 200KB

A 200 KB limit gives you more room than 50 or 100 KB, so photos and document scans stay clearer. It is a frequent limit for photo and document fields on online forms. This tool keeps the highest quality that still fits under 200 KB and only resizes the picture when it has to. It works for clear photos, certificates and scanned documents.

  1. Choose your picture: Click "Browse Image" or drop a JPG, PNG or WebP file onto the box.
  2. Wait a moment: The tool tries different sizes and qualities until the file is smaller than 200 KB.
  3. Check the preview: You see the original and the new picture side by side with both file sizes.
  4. Download: Click the download button. Your original file is never changed.

Good to Know

If your picture is already fairly small, it may keep its full pixel size at 200 KB. Only very large photos need resizing to fit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I compress an image to 200KB?

Choose your image, wait a moment, and download the result. The tool automatically finds the best quality that keeps the file under 200 KB. You do not need to move any sliders.

Will my picture still look good under 200KB?

The tool keeps the highest quality that fits inside the limit, but a strict size limit always costs some detail. Large photos are made smaller in pixels first, because that loses less quality than squeezing a huge picture. Check the preview before you download.

Why was my picture made smaller in pixels?

File size depends on how many pixels a picture has. A multi-megapixel phone photo cannot fit in 200 KB at full size without looking bad, so the tool reduces the pixel size just enough to fit. The result box tells you the exact new size.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Everything happens inside your browser on your own device. Your picture is never sent to Fixlytools or anyone else.

Is the result really under 200 KB on every website?

The tool keeps the file under 200,000 bytes, which is below 200 KB whether a website counts 1 KB as 1,000 or 1,024 bytes. The downloaded file name ends with -200kb.

What format is the downloaded image?

A JPG image, which is accepted by most upload forms. PNG and WebP pictures are converted to JPG, and transparent areas become white.
