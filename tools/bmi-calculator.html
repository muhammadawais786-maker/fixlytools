How to Calculate Your BMI

Body mass index (BMI) compares your weight with your height. It is a quick screening number used by doctors and health organizations. This calculator shows your BMI, your category and the weight range that would be considered normal for your height.

  1. Choose your units: Use Metric for centimeters and kilograms, or Imperial for feet, inches and pounds.
  2. Enter your height and weight: Type your height and your current weight.
  3. Pick the cut-offs: Leave the box unticked for the standard WHO categories. Tick it to use the lower WHO Asia-Pacific cut-offs often used for South and East Asian adults.
  4. Press Calculate BMI: See your BMI, your category on the colour scale and the normal weight range for your height.

Key Features

  • Metric and imperial: Works with cm and kg, or feet, inches and pounds.
  • WHO and Asian cut-offs: Standard categories, plus the WHO Asia-Pacific cut-offs (overweight from 23, obese from 27.5).
  • Healthy weight range: Shows the weight range that keeps your BMI in the normal band for your height.
  • Clear colour scale: A bar shows where your BMI sits between underweight and obese.
  • Private: Your height and weight never leave your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is BMI calculated?

BMI = weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. A person who is 1.70 m tall and weighs 70 kg has a BMI of 70 / (1.70 x 1.70) = 24.2.

What are the BMI categories?

The WHO categories for adults are: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 normal, 25 to 29.9 overweight, and 30 or more obese (split into classes I, II and III from 30, 35 and 40).

Why is there an Asian BMI option?

The WHO Asia-Pacific guidance uses lower cut-offs because health risks can rise at a lower BMI in many Asian populations. Under it, 23 to 27.4 is overweight and 27.5 or more is obese.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so a muscular person can have a high BMI without being overweight. BMI also does not show where fat is stored.

Can I use it for children?

No. Children and teenagers are assessed with growth charts and BMI percentiles for their age and sex. Please ask a doctor.

Is this medical advice?

No. BMI is only a screening number. For any health decision, such as dieting or weight loss, please talk to a doctor or a qualified dietitian.
