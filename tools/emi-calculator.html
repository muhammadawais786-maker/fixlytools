How to Calculate Your Loan EMI Online

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan — covering both principal and interest. Before taking a home loan, car loan, or personal loan, it is important to know exactly how much you will pay each month and the total interest over the full loan term. Fixlytools EMI Calculator gives you an instant, accurate breakdown.

  1. Enter Loan Amount: Type the total loan amount (principal) you wish to borrow.
  2. Enter Interest Rate: Enter the annual interest rate offered by your bank or lender (for example, 8.5%).
  3. Enter Loan Tenure: Enter the loan repayment period in months or years (for example, 20 years or 240 months).
  4. Calculate: Click "Calculate EMI" to instantly see your monthly payment, total amount payable, and total interest.
  5. See the Split: A colour bar shows the proportion of principal versus interest in your total repayment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is a fixed monthly payment made by a borrower to a lender on a specific date each month. Each EMI payment partially repays the principal amount borrowed and partially covers the interest accrued.

How is EMI calculated?

The standard EMI formula is: EMI = P x R x (1+R)^N / ((1+R)^N - 1), where P is the principal loan amount, R is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and N is the total number of monthly payments.

What happens if I make extra payments?

Making extra principal payments reduces your outstanding loan balance, which reduces the total interest paid over the loan term. This calculator assumes standard fixed instalments without extra payments.

Can I use this for home loans, car loans and personal loans?

Yes. The EMI formula is the same for all types of term loans. Simply enter the correct loan amount, interest rate, and tenure for your specific loan.

What is a good EMI to salary ratio?

Financial advisors generally recommend that your total monthly EMI payments should not exceed 40–50% of your monthly take-home salary. A lower ratio leaves you with more financial flexibility.
